\begin{abstract}
Pretrained autoregressive Transformers embody enormous sunk compute. We ask whether that compute survives changing \emph{both} the architecture (attention $\to$ recurrent state) and the generative objective (next-token $\to$ iterative denoising); prior conversion work crosses one axis at a time. We convert Qwen3 teachers at $1.7$B and $8$B into attention-free bidirectional gated-delta-rule diffusion students in three stages---attention-to-recurrence distillation, bidirectional expansion, and diffusion continual pretraining---so that each capability can be traced to the stage that kept or lost it. Language modeling transfers. In-context retrieval does not: on a teacher-gated multi-query recall probe the converted students score exactly $0.000$ at both scales, and diffusion pretraining alone does not restore it. A table-to-query distance curriculum in the final stage repairs it, but only stochastically: under an open-loop schedule one seed in three learns to retrieve. Making the schedule closed-loop---advancing the retrieval distance only while a running accuracy estimate stays above threshold---lifts the same three seeds to $3/3$ at $0.94$--$0.99$, holds on real text ($0.955$--$0.995$ on every cell), and carries unchanged to $8$B ($0.000 \to 0.901$ mean, generalizing to twice the trained load). The remaining $8$B misses trace to a heavy-tailed bootstrap onset ($6.5$k, $11$k, ${>}16$k steps) that fixed step budgets truncate. One boundary survives every intervention: every model that solves its trained token band reads chance on held-out bands, and a role-randomizing arm shows the gap is token \emph{coverage}, not memorized bindings. We also complete a block-diffusion conversion of a $7$B code model into a $3{:}1$ recurrent--attention hybrid ($85$k steps, divergence-free): held-out NLL $2.819$, with two negative results from its training.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Two conversion literatures exist and they do not intersect. One adapts pretrained autoregressive Transformers into \emph{diffusion} language models while keeping the Transformer backbone \citep{gong2025diffullama,ye2025dream}. The other distills attention into \emph{linear-time recurrence} while keeping next-token prediction \citep{goldstein2025radlads}. An attention-free diffusion LM built from a pretrained Transformer requires both moves, and to our knowledge no one has reported crossing both.

We do so in three stages, each with its own objective and its own frozen set (\S\ref{sec:pipeline}): distillation of the attention teacher into a causal recurrent student, expansion of that student into a bidirectional denoiser, and continual pretraining under a masked-diffusion objective. The staging is the method. When a capability fails to transfer, we can say at which stage it was lost.

The finding that organizes the paper is a dissociation. Converted students keep what the teacher \emph{knows} and lose what the teacher can \emph{do within a sequence}. On a multi-query associative recall probe (\S\ref{sec:probe}) where the attention teacher scores $0.335$--$0.350$, the converted student scores $0.000$ at both $1.7$B and $8$B---not degraded, absent---and diffusion pretraining alone does not bring it back. This is the interference wall our companion paper isolates on a toy bench \citep{anatomyrecall}, where a table-to-query distance curriculum removes it. We port that curriculum into the final stage.

The port works, but stochastically, and the paper's main practical result is what makes it reliable. Under an \emph{open-loop} curriculum, with retrieval distance advancing on a fixed schedule, one seed in three learns to retrieve. Gating the schedule on measured retrieval accuracy, so that distance advances only while the model is keeping up, takes the same three seeds to $3/3$, and the resulting recipe carries unchanged to $8$B.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{A staged conversion pipeline across both axes} (\S\ref{sec:pipeline}), and a stage-attributable account of what transfers: language modeling does; in-context retrieval does not ($0.000$ at $1.7$B and $8$B; \S\ref{sec:transfer}).
\item \textbf{A closed-loop retrieval curriculum} (\S\ref{sec:stage3}, \S\ref{sec:lottery}). Under an open-loop distance ramp the repair lands on one seed in three. A success-gated ramp lifts the same seeds to $3/3$ at $0.94$--$0.99$ and holds on real text ($0.955$--$0.995$ on every evaluated cell).
\item \textbf{Transfer to 8B, and the residual failure mode} (\S\ref{sec:8b}). Recall transfer rises from $0.000$ to a mean of $0.901$, generalizing to twice the trained pair load and beyond the trained distance. Two of three seeds lock in; the miss is a heavy-tailed bootstrap onset ($6.5$k, $11$k, ${>}16$k steps) that a fixed step budget truncates.
\item \textbf{A token-coverage boundary} (\S\ref{sec:coverage}). Every model that solves its trained token band reads chance on held-out bands, at both scales. An arm that randomizes key/value roles every batch rules out memorized bindings as the cause; three curriculum policies over randomized pools fail by three distinct mechanisms.
\item \textbf{A completed block-diffusion conversion} (\S\ref{sec:blockdiff}): a $7$B code model into a $3{:}1$ recurrent--attention hybrid, trained to $85{,}449$+$4{,}000$ steps divergence-free (held-out NLL $2.819$), with its construction and two negative results from training---a representation-alignment auxiliary that does nothing, and a learning-rate ceiling that appears only late in training.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{AR-to-diffusion adaptation.} \citet{gong2025diffullama} and \citet{ye2025dream} adapt pretrained autoregressive Transformers into masked diffusion LMs, establishing that the objective change is survivable and far cheaper than pretraining. Both retain attention. \textbf{Attention-to-recurrence distillation.} RADLADS \citep{goldstein2025radlads} converts attention into RWKV-7-style recurrence at multi-billion scale on a few hundred million tokens, retaining next-token prediction; our stage 1 follows its two-phase recipe. \textbf{Recurrent denoisers.} DiffuMamba \citep{singh2025diffumamba} shows that scan backbones are viable denoisers; the rank-1 state update of \citet{peng2025rwkv7} and \citet{yang2024gateddeltanet} is the cell we convert into. \textbf{Block diffusion} \citep{arriola2025bd3lm} interpolates between the autoregressive and diffusion objectives and is the objective of the conversion in \S\ref{sec:blockdiff}. \textbf{Recall in recurrence.} That fixed-state recurrences underperform attention on associative recall is well documented \citep{arora2023zoology,arora2024based}. That the deficit is substantially a \emph{learnability} problem rather than a capacity limit is the reading our companion paper argues on a toy bench \citep{anatomyrecall}, with convergent evidence elsewhere \citep{okpekpe2025recall,blouir2024birdie}; this paper tests it at scale.

\section{Method: The Conversion Pipeline}\label{sec:pipeline}

The pipeline takes an attention teacher to an attention-free bidirectional recurrent diffusion student in three stages. Table~\ref{tab:settings} lists the concrete settings at both scales.

\subsection{Stage 1: attention to recurrence}\label{sec:stage1}

The teacher is a uniform softmax Transformer: Qwen3-1.7B (28 layers) or Qwen3-8B (36 layers, hidden size $4096$, 32 query and 8 key--value heads). The student inherits the teacher's token embeddings, MLPs, normalization layers and output head, all frozen, and replaces \emph{every} attention sublayer with a gated delta-rule recurrent cell \citep{yang2024gateddeltanet}. Training follows RADLADS \citep{goldstein2025radlads} in two phases. In \emph{attention transfer}, each cell is trained in isolation, on the teacher's own hidden states, to reproduce the output of the attention sublayer it replaces. In \emph{refinement}, the assembled student is trained end-to-end to match the teacher's next-token distribution (logit KL). The teacher is served in 8-bit. The corpus is text8 (95M characters of lowercase Wikipedia text) at sequence length 256; the refinement budget is 60k steps (30.7M tokens) at $1.7$B and 120k steps (${\approx}30$M tokens) at $8$B. Stage-1 quality is reported as teacher top-1 agreement on held-out text ($0.746$ at $1.7$B, $0.779$ at $8$B) and perplexity retention ($0.932$ at $1.7$B). The objective is still next-token prediction; only the architecture has moved.

\subsection{Stage 2: bidirectional expansion}\label{sec:stage2}

Each converted layer gains a \emph{backward} cell, initialized as a copy of its forward cell and run over the reversed sequence, and a merge $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times 2d}$ that combines the two streams, initialized to their average. The embedding table gains one row for a mask token. This stage is structural: it changes the network's shape without changing its function, and the new parameters are learned in stage 3. Bidirectionality is what a denoiser requires---every position must see both sides of the sequence---and it is also what gives the student query-first reading for free: the backward stream reads a query before the text that precedes it.

\subsection{Stage 3: diffusion continual pretraining, with a retrieval curriculum}\label{sec:stage3}

\textbf{Objective.} Masked discrete diffusion \citep{austin2021d3pm,sahoo2024mdlm}: a fraction of tokens is replaced by the mask token and the model is trained to fill them, with the mask ratio annealed from $0.15$ to $0.65$ over training. By default the trainable set is the backward cells, the merges and the mask embedding, with the forward cells frozen at their stage-1 values. The \trainfwd{} variant also unfreezes the forward cells. Both are used below, because the contrast between them is informative (\S\ref{sec:transfer}).

\textbf{Retrieval episodes.} With probability $p$ a training batch is a retrieval episode rather than a text batch. An episode has the layout
\begin{center}\small
$[\text{text prefix}]\;[k_1\,v_1\;\cdots\;k_N\,v_N]\;[\text{text haystack, } g \text{ tokens}]$\\[2pt]
$[k_j\;\texttt{MASK}]\;[k_{j'}\;\texttt{MASK}]\;\cdots$
\end{center}
with $N \in \{4, 8, 16\}$ pairs and four queried pairs per episode, every answer masked and supervised. Keys and values are single frequent tokens of the corpus, 64 of each, fixed for the run; the prefix and haystack are real text. The haystack length $g$ is the curriculum variable: each batch samples $g$ uniformly in $[0, \text{cap}]$, and the cap grows from $0$ toward the maximum that fits the sequence. Evaluation is always at the largest gaps.

\textbf{Schedule: open-loop vs.\ closed-loop.} In the \emph{open-loop} (ramp) schedule the cap grows linearly with the step count, reaching its maximum at 80\% of training. In the \emph{closed-loop} (success-gated) schedule an exponential moving average (EMA) of retrieval accuracy is maintained over retrieval batches, and the cap advances by a fixed increment only on steps where the EMA is at or above a threshold ($0.6$). The cap is a ratchet: it never retreats. A \emph{thermostat} variant, used in \S\ref{sec:coverage}, lets the cap retreat while the EMA is below threshold.

\textbf{Randomized symbol pool.} For the coverage experiments of \S\ref{sec:coverage}, keys and values are drawn afresh for every batch from a pool of $P$ tokens ($P \in \{128, 512\}$) rather than a fixed set of 64, so that no particular binding can be memorized. Pool-size \emph{annealing} advances through tiers ($128 \to 512 \to 2048$) after a streak of passing steps.

\subsection{Evaluation: a teacher-gated recall probe}\label{sec:probe}

Retrieval is measured with a multi-query associative recall probe in the episode format above: $N \in \{4, 8, 16\}$ pairs, a real-text haystack of $g \in \{0, 64, 160\}$ tokens, and one masked answer position scored per episode by exact match. The value marginal, ${\approx}0.023$, is chance. Two controls accompany every number. \emph{Shuffled}: the bindings are permuted and the original value is scored, so a genuine retriever must collapse to the marginal. \emph{Mismatch}: the query is a key that never appeared, which gives the prior floor. The probe format was validated on the attention teacher before any student was scored: the teacher must clear the marginal by a wide margin and collapse under shuffling. This \emph{teacher gate} rules out probe artifacts as an explanation for student failures.

\textbf{Trained band and disjoint band.} The \disj{} evaluation is the identical probe with keys and values drawn from a token band that never appears in any retrieval episode during training and is excluded from every randomized pool. It asks whether what was learned is retrieval, or retrieval over particular tokens.

\textbf{Lock-in.} Training telemetry makes learning to retrieve a binary event. The retrieval EMA sits near zero for thousands of steps and then rises past $0.6$ within roughly a thousand steps; we call this the \emph{avalanche}, and a run that avalanches within its budget \emph{locked in}. Every locked-in run's evaluation clears both controls. Because the event is binary, we report lock-in rates with per-seed values rather than means.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{What transfers, and what does not}\label{sec:transfer}

Stage 1 transfers language modeling: top-1 agreement with the teacher of $0.746$ and $0.779$ on held-out text. It does not transfer in-context retrieval. On the recall probe the attention teacher scores $0.335$--$0.350$; the converted student scores $0.000$ at both $1.7$B and $8$B, and its accuracy is insensitive to whether the bindings are shuffled---it is emitting a prior, not retrieving. Under stage-3 diffusion pretraining alone (no retrieval episodes) it remains at $0.000$. The capacity exists---the same cell trained from scratch on the toy bench solves 16-pair recall at $0.996$ \citep{anatomyrecall}---but the distillation did not instill it.

\textbf{The curriculum needs trainable forward cells.} Injecting retrieval episodes into stage 3 with the forward cells frozen teaches the model to emit well-formed answers but never to bind: accuracy stays at the prior. With the forward cells unfrozen (\trainfwd), retrieval is acquired. This is what the mechanism account predicts. If the circuit is \emph{mark-and-route}---the backward stream marks the binding as it passes the query, the forward stream routes it to the answer position---then the routing direction must be trainable. The cost is a small denoising penalty against the no-curriculum control ($-0.06$ masked-token accuracy).

Under the open-loop ramp at $1.7$B, three seeds draw $\{0.000, 0.000, 0.40\text{--}0.70\}$: one partial lock-in in three (Table~\ref{tab:atscale}). An earlier unseeded pilot run of the same configuration had reached $0.94$--$1.00$; an exact rerun returned $0.000$, and we count the pilot as one more draw from the same lottery rather than as a result. The same lesson arrived independently on the toy bench, where a two-seed directional margin reversed under ten-seed replication \citep{anatomyrecall}: in this training regime, low-seed means are not evidence.

\textbf{Why the open-loop schedule fails.} The ramp advances the gap cap on a clock. A run that has not yet learned to bind at short gaps is dragged to long ones regardless, and spends the rest of its budget training at distances it cannot solve. Whether a seed escapes before the ramp outpaces it is close to a coin flip.

\textbf{The closed-loop schedule.} Gating the cap on the retrieval EMA removes the failure: no run is advanced while failing, and a run that stalls simply trains longer where it is. On the same three seeds the gated ramp locks in $3/3$, with per-seed means $0.971$, $0.977$ and $0.974$, accuracy at the maximum evaluated gap matching gap zero, and both controls clean. The telemetry matches the mechanism: all three runs climbed the ramp with the EMA at $0.98$--$0.99$ throughout. Their final caps ($0.66$--$0.87$ of the maximum) never reached $1.0$, yet evaluation is already at ceiling at the maximum gap; the tail of the ramp is unnecessary at this sequence length.

\textbf{Real text.} On wikitext the gated recipe reaches $0.955$--$0.995$ on every $N \times \text{gap}$ cell, including $N{=}16$ at gap $160$ ($0.975$), with clean controls. Its telemetry shows why earlier real-corpus attempts had failed: bootstrap is slower on real text (EMA $0.04$ at 3k steps, avalanche by 5.4k, a clean ramp to cap $0.91$), so open-loop schedules outran it. Real text delays lock-in; it does not prevent it. The conversion-scale recipe is therefore \emph{curriculum $+$ trainable forward cells $+$ success-gated ramp}, and the claim it supports is a lock-in rate.

\subsection{Scaling to 8B}\label{sec:8b}

Applied unchanged to the $8$B student, the recipe lifts recall transfer from $0.000$ to $0.69$--$0.99$ (mean $0.901$) on the evaluation grid, with a bidirectional denoising gain of $+0.117$ over the causal stream. A wider evaluation of the same model holds at twice the trained pair load and past the trained distance ($N \le 32$, gap $\le 224$: $0.49$--$0.99$, mean $0.786$), so both capacity and distance generalize beyond the training distribution.

\textbf{The residual lottery is bootstrap onset.} Across three $8$B seeds the tally is $2/3$. Every locked-in run, at both scales, shows the same profile: a long dead phase, an avalanche in which retrieval NLL collapses within about a thousand steps, then a clean gated ramp. What varies is \emph{when}. Observed onsets are $6.5$k, $11$k and ${>}16$k steps at $8$B, $13$k for the randomized-pool arm and $5$k for the wikitext arm at $1.7$B. The distribution is heavy-tailed, and every run here had a fixed 16k-step budget, so the budget truncates the tail. The amplitudes show the same effect: seed 1 avalanched at $11$k and finished with a weaker floor (worst cell $0.35$) than seed 0, which avalanched at $6.5$k (worst cell $0.69$), because later onset leaves less post-avalanche training. The indicated fix is a success-gated \emph{budget}---train until the avalanche, then a fixed number of steps beyond it---rather than a larger fixed total. It is designed and not yet run; we report $2/3$ as it stands.

\textbf{A second cell family.} An RWKV-7 student at $8$B trains stably under the same recipe, with a positive bidirectional denoising gain ($+0.053$), but does not avalanche within $16$k steps. Given the onset distribution above we read this as underpowered rather than negative: RWKV-7's retrieval bootstrap is slower than the gated delta-rule cell's everywhere we have measured it, and a verdict needs the gated budget.

\subsection{What does not transfer: token coverage}\label{sec:coverage}

Every model that solves the trained-band evaluation---at $1.7$B and $8$B, including all three gated-ramp seeds and the wikitext arm---scores $0.000$ on the \disj{} band. What the curriculum installs is a binding circuit over the symbols that appeared in retrieval episodes, not an abstract retrieval faculty.

The obvious explanation is memorization of fixed bindings, and it is wrong. An arm trained with keys and values drawn afresh from a $128$-token pool every batch, so that no fixed binding \emph{can} be memorized, still locks in (late, at ${\approx}13$k steps, with the same avalanche profile), reaches $0.39$--$0.92$ on its trained band, and still reads $0.000$ on the disjoint band. It also generalizes in distance far beyond its own training: its cap reached only ${\approx}40$ tokens, yet gap-$64$ accuracy matches gap-$0$ and gap-$160$ sits well above chance. The circuit is neither memorizing pairs nor distance-bound. The gap is which \emph{embeddings} ever participated in a retrieval episode.

\textbf{Three failure modes, one cause.} Randomizing the pool interacts with the gate, and mapping the interaction was necessary to avoid mistaking it for a refutation. At threshold $0.6$ the pool task starts below threshold, the cap never advances, and the arm trains only at gap ${\approx}0$ (\emph{lockout}). At threshold $0.25$ the cap advances on a weak signal and the EMA then collapses; because the cap is a ratchet, all subsequent training runs at gaps the model cannot solve and the circuit never recovers (\emph{learn-then-collapse}). With the thermostat, a $512$-token pool never bootstraps within budget while a $128$-token pool does, at $13$k steps (\emph{non-bootstrap}). Three policies, three mechanisms, one cause: the randomized-pool task has a much later bootstrap onset, and every policy we tried was budget-limited rather than policy-limited.

\textbf{Pool annealing is untested.} The diagnosis points at pool-size annealing: ride the avalanche on a narrow pool, then widen coverage. At $8$B the one annealing run never bootstrapped its first tier inside a $24$k-step budget, so the tiers never engaged. Annealing is therefore untested rather than refuted, and it is blocked on the same success-gated budget that \S\ref{sec:8b} identifies.

\textbf{A hybrid alternative.} Because token coverage resisted every training-side intervention we tried, retaining a sparse set of attention layers is worth naming as a third reason for hybrids, alongside capacity and length. An attention-retaining variant of the pipeline (keeping every fourth attention layer) passes end-to-end validation on a small teacher, and \S\ref{sec:blockdiff} uses it at $7$B; its retrieval has not yet been measured.

\section{Block-Diffusion Conversion of a 7B Code Model}\label{sec:blockdiff}

The conversions above use full-sequence masked diffusion. A block-diffusion objective \citep{arriola2025bd3lm}, which denoises within fixed-size blocks conditioned on committed earlier blocks, interpolates toward autoregression and is a better match for an instruction-following target. We converted a Qwen2.5-Coder-7B teacher under it, and the run completed cleanly: $85{,}449$ pretraining steps ($700$M tokens) followed by $4{,}000$ response-masked SFT steps, both phases divergence-free under the guard stack. \textbf{Outcome}: held-out block-diffusion NLL $2.819$ (masked accuracy $0.512$; the training-objective EMA closed at $2.786$, a $0.03$-nat generalization gap), against a teacher-relative clean-stream AR ratio of $5.4\times$ (student $2.96$ vs.\ teacher $1.27$) on fresh documents. The conversion is therefore a working block-diffusion LM with a measured language-modeling floor, but its in-context retrieval and instruction-following remain at the pre-SFT level --- the retrieval curriculum was not part of this leg, and the AR ratio says the hybrid still models noticeably worse than its teacher. Generation under blockwise sampling was blocked on our eval hardware by a compiler fault; we report construction and training health rather than samples. We also report the construction, because it differs from the pipeline above in ways that matter, and two negative results its training produced.

\textbf{Architecture.} The teacher has 28 layers (hidden size $3584$; 28 query and 4 key--value heads). The student replaces 21 of its attention layers with gated delta-rule cells and keeps every fourth attention layer as-is, a $3{:}1$ recurrent--attention hybrid. The cells are teacher-matched: 28 heads of key dimension $128$ and value dimension $256$, with the query and output projections copied directly from the replaced attention layer and the key/value projections tiled from its grouped-query heads. The kept attention layers are trainable and initialized as exact copies. Embeddings, MLPs, norms and the output head are inherited; MLPs, norms and the head stay frozen. Trainable parameters total $2.91$B. Stage 1 is as in \S\ref{sec:stage1}, with the copied initialization replacing random initialization and refinement stopped on an agreement plateau. Stage 2 is not needed: block diffusion is bidirectional only within a block, and the within-block context is provided by the noised stream below rather than by a backward recurrence.

\textbf{Two-stream training.} Each step builds a clean and a noised view of the same batch. The clean stream is an ordinary causal pass of the network. The noised stream conditions, per 64-token block, on the clean stream's state for strictly-previous blocks and on itself within the block. Setting the diffusion block equal to the recurrent chunk size makes this nearly free: the chunked recurrence already materializes each chunk's incoming state, so exposing it yields ``clean recurrent state at the start of block $n$'' at no extra forward cost, and the noised stream needs no scan of its own. Kept attention layers attend, in the noised pass, to clean keys from previous blocks and noised keys within the block. Per-block noise levels are drawn uniformly on $[0.2, 1]$ with the usual $1/t$ weighting, and the output is shifted by one position so that the teacher's next-token head semantics are preserved.

\textbf{Budget.} Sequence length 512, $8192$ tokens per step, 85{,}449 pretraining steps (700M tokens from a 2.1B-token code-and-text corpus) on a single TPU v5e-8 host, followed by a 4{,}000-step supervised phase that denoises only the response region of chat-formatted examples ($512{\times}16$, response mask ratio $\sim U[0.3,1.0]$, ten-percent corpus regularizer).

\textbf{Negative result 1: a representation-alignment auxiliary does nothing.} We added a term aligning the student's clean-stream hidden states to the frozen teacher's at each layer boundary, expecting it to preserve teacher structure through the objective change. Measured, the cosine alignment was already ${\approx}94\%$ saturated at initialization (the copied projections and frozen backbone anchor the hidden states on their own), autoregressive retention did not improve, logit scale inflated roughly twofold, and the term cost $10$--$15\%$ of step time. It is off.

\textbf{Negative result 2: the learning-rate ceiling appears late.} At learning rate $3{\times}10^{-4}$ the run diverged twice, both times deep into training (around steps $50$--$52$k and $56$k) and both times with the same signature: escalating oscillation in loss over several hundred steps, then spike, recover, collapse, with accuracy falling to ${\approx}0.02$. No single step exceeded the loss-spike guard's threshold, so a step-level guard registered nothing. Training at $2{\times}10^{-4}$ is stable, as it was for the $1.5$B validation run that preceded this one. The failure is invisible to the obvious monitor and appears only after tens of thousands of stable steps.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Token generality is unsolved.} Every solved model reads $0.000$ on held-out token bands (\S\ref{sec:coverage}). We make no claim that the curriculum installs a general retrieval faculty; the annealing fix that our own diagnosis motivates is untested.
\item \textbf{Three seeds per setting.} Conversion-scale rows rest on three seeds (one for the wikitext arm). $3/3$, $2/3$ and $1/1$ are small-$n$ rates, and no pairwise contrast in this paper is significance-tested.
\item \textbf{Fixed budgets confound several negatives.} The $8$B seed-2 miss, the RWKV-7 non-bootstrap and the pool-annealing arm are all consistent with budget truncation of a heavy-tailed onset rather than method failure. Until the success-gated budget is run, those three are open.
\item \textbf{One probe family.} Retrieval is measured with a single multi-query recall probe whose format is adjacent to the training episodes. Held-out bindings and a held-out token band are used, but the format proximity is real, and we do not claim broad downstream retrieval.
\item \textbf{The block-diffusion conversion is complete but not yet chat-capable} (\S\ref{sec:blockdiff}): training finished cleanly with a measured holdout floor, but generation sampling, retrieval transfer, and instruction-following evaluation are open; the $5.4\times$ AR ratio marks a real gap to the teacher.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What conversion costs.} Teacher knowledge survives crossing both axes; the in-sequence binding faculty survives neither. It is not degraded by conversion but absent, and continual pretraining under the diffusion objective does not bring it back. It has to be retrained deliberately, and the toy-bench mechanism account \citep{anatomyrecall} says why: the wall is a training-coverage gap, so only training that covers the gap can close it.

\textbf{Closed-loop curricula are the transferable lesson.} An open-loop curriculum is a clock; a closed-loop one is a controller; the difference is $1/3$ versus $3/3$ on identical seeds. The same distinction predicts the toy bench's length boundary, where a time-based ramp collapses to $0/6$ at $L{=}512$ \citep{anatomyrecall}. Wherever a curriculum has a difficulty knob and a measurable competence signal, gating the former on the latter converts a lottery into a method. The natural extension, and the one all of our open negatives point at, is to gate the training \emph{budget} the same way: a fixed step count is itself an open-loop decision about a heavy-tailed random variable.

\textbf{What the coverage boundary means.} A converted model whose retrieval was trained over a narrow symbol pool reads chance outside it, and this survived pool randomization, three gate policies and a scale change. Curriculum-installed retrieval is a circuit over participating embeddings, which makes token coverage a first-class axis of the training distribution. It also supplies a third reason to keep some attention in a hybrid, alongside capacity and length.

\section*{Reproducibility Statement}
Per-arm training telemetry (gate cap, retrieval EMA, avalanche onset), evaluation outputs for every arm including the failed ones, and checkpoints for every arm except the unseeded pilot are retained in object storage. The toy-bench code and results referenced throughout are public at \url{https://github.com/JIBSIL/dualgoose}.

\section*{Impact Statement}
This work concerns making pretrained language models cheaper to re-architect, which lowers the compute cost of deploying capable models and correspondingly lowers the barrier to deploying them without scrutiny. The retrieval boundary we report is also safety-relevant: a converted model can look competent on its training distribution and read at chance just outside it, a failure mode that perplexity-shaped evaluation does not surface.
